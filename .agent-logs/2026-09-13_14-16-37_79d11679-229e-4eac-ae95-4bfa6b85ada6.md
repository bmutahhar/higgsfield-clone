**How it works.** A single document-level capture-phase click listener arms the bar, and `usePathname()` disarms it. Capture phase matters: `<Link>` calls `preventDefault()` on its way out, so by the time a click bubbles to the document every real client navigation looks cancelled. `usePathname` only advances once the navigation Transition commits, which makes it the *end* of the navigation rather than the start of the request. `popstate` covers back/forward.

One listener rather than `useLinkStatus`, which only reports on the `<Link>` it's nested inside and would have meant routing all 9 link sites through a wrapper.

Following the `use-compact-header` idiom, the three phases are written straight to the DOM as `data-state` and drawn entirely in CSS — a navigation re-renders nothing but that one leaf.

**Two things I changed from my first pass, both caught in the browser:**

1. Tailwind v4 compiles `scale-x-*` to the standalone `scale` property, not `transform`. My keyframes animated `transform: scaleX()`, and the two *multiply* — the utility's `scale: 0 1` would have pinned the bar shut permanently. Keyframes are now written against `scale`.
2. I'd pinned the bar to the top of the viewport, where it sat invisibly on the lime promo strip — lime on lime, in the default state. It now hangs from a zero-height slot in the flow, drawing over the first 3px of the dark header, and rides up on its own when the strip is dismissed.
